Guitar note analyser

Record a note at each gain setting. Each note's crest factor (peak ÷ RMS) is measured, and the notes are tested for whether the crest factor follows the knob. Clipping flattens the peaks, so more gain should mean a lower crest factor.

What the terms mean
Sample
One number in a recording: the signal's voltage at one instant, scaled so that ±1 is the loudest the file can hold.
Sample rate
How many samples are taken each second, usually 44,100 or 48,000.
dBFS
Decibels relative to full scale. 0 dBFS is the loudest a recording can be; −12 dBFS is a quarter of that in amplitude.
Peak
The biggest swing away from zero in the measured window, ignoring whether it is up or down.
RMS
Root mean square: square every sample, average the squares, take the square root. The note’s effective level, roughly how loud it sounds.
Crest factor
Peak ÷ RMS, given in decibels. High (about 8 dB) for a clean plucked note, whose peaks are sharp; 0 dB for a square wave, whose every sample is at the peak. Clipping flattens the peaks, so it lowers the crest factor.
Decibel (dB)
A scale for ratios on which equal steps are equal proportions. For amplitudes, dB = 20 × log10(ratio): a ratio of 2 is 6.02 dB, 1 is 0 dB.
Onset
Where the pluck begins: the first 5 ms slice whose level reaches 10% of the loudest slice.
Analysis window
The 300 ms of each note that is measured, starting 50 ms after the onset, past the click of the pick.
Rank
A value’s place in order, smallest first. Tied values share the average of the places they fill.
Spearman’s ρ (rho)
How consistently one ranking follows another, from −1 (perfectly opposite) through 0 (unrelated) to +1 (perfectly together).
p-value
The chance of a relationship at least this strong if the gain knob made no difference at all. Below 0.05 is usually taken as unlikely to be chance.
95% interval
A range that, judged by resampling your own notes, probably holds the true ρ. Narrower with more notes.
Standard deviation
How far a single note typically sits from the average at its setting: mostly how consistently you plucked.
Standard error
Standard deviation ÷ √(number of notes): how precisely the average is known. The error bars on the gain chart are ± one standard error.
Excluded note
A note kept in the database but left out of the analysis. Untick and tick again in the notes table.

How to record
  1. Plug the pedal's output into your audio interface and choose it under Input.
  2. Use one note throughout (say the open A string), plucked the same way each time.
  3. Press Record, pluck once, and let the note ring. One note per recording.
  4. Record about five notes at each gain setting, then move the knob and repeat.
  5. Keep the interface's input below −6 dBFS: clipping in the interface flattens peaks exactly as the pedal does and would count as extra distortion.

The browser's echo cancellation, noise suppression and automatic gain control are switched off for recording, since each of them would change the waveform being measured.
